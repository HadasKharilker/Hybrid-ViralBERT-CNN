Hybrid ViralBERT-CNN: A Multi-Modal Approach for Enhanced Virality Prediction of K-pop Tweets

Abstract & Motivation

The K-pop industry represents a unique social media phenomenon with highly engaged fan communities capable of rapidly amplifying content. This research addresses the challenge of predicting tweet virality in the K-pop domain by developing a hybrid model that combines ViralBERT's dual-BERT architecture with CNN-based feature extraction.

Research Gap: Existing models (ViralBERT: 49% accuracy, CNN-IndoBERT: 91.4% on Indonesian data) lack domain-specific adaptation for K-pop English Twitter content.

Dataset: 9,251 curated English K-pop tweets collected over two months, featuring comprehensive engagement metrics and user characteristics.

Methodology & Innovation

Dual-Phase Experimental Design

Phase 1: Architectural modifications to ViralBERT with CNN integration

Phase 2: Integration of CNN-IndoBERT's advanced preprocessing methodology

Hybrid Architecture
BERTweet (768D) + RoBERTa (3D) → CNN Layers → MLP Classification

Key Innovation: Cross-linguistic adaptation of CNN-IndoBERT preprocessing pipeline from Indonesian to English K-pop Twitter data.

  • HTML tag and entity removal
  • URL and mention normalization
  • Special character standardization
  • Porter stemming with hashtag preservation
  • Stop-word removal

Related Work

ViralBERT (Rameez et al., 2022)

BERTweet + RoBERTa + MLP

Results: 49.4% accuracy, 52.3% F1-score on multi-domain English tweets

CNN-IndoBERT (Benedecas & Girsang, 2024)

IndoBERT + 1D CNN + Advanced preprocessing

Results: 91.4% accuracy, 80.3% F1-score on Indonesian tweets

Key Insight: High-quality preprocessing can be more impactful than architectural complexity for social media text analysis.

Key Findings

  • Data Quality > Architecture Complexity: Advanced preprocessing yielded dramatically better results than architectural modifications
  • Cross-linguistic Adaptation Success: CNN-IndoBERT methodology successfully adapted from Indonesian to English K-pop content
  • Domain-Specific Performance: K-pop Twitter data achieved exceptional results (99.8% F1-score) without requiring domain-specific features
  • Semantic Pattern Recognition: Clean preprocessing allowed BERT models to focus on meaningful virality patterns rather than surface variations
The CNN architecture was fundamentally sound but data-starved rather than architecturally limited.

Conclusions & Future Work

Major Contribution: Demonstrated that advanced preprocessing methodologies can be more impactful than complex architectural designs for social media virality prediction.

Limitations & Future Research:

  • Cross-domain validation needed on diverse English Twitter content
  • Investigation of K-pop linguistic patterns contributing to success
  • Analysis of Korean-English code-switching effects
  • Exploration of cultural elements with universal virality characteristics
Impact: This work extends CNN-IndoBERT findings beyond Indonesian data and provides a robust framework for domain-specific virality prediction.
